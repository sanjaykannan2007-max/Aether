01 · Foundations

From bits to qubits

A classical bit is either 0 or 1. A qubit can exist in a superposition of both — until measured.

The Qubit State

Unlike classical bits, a qubit |ψ⟩ is a complex linear combination of the basis states |0⟩ and |1⟩. Two real parameters fully describe it: the polar angle θ and the azimuthal angle φ.

|ψ⟩ = cos(θ/2)|0⟩ + eiφsin(θ/2)|1⟩
θ ∈ [0, π]  ·  φ ∈ [0, 2π]

The Bloch Sphere

Felix Bloch's geometric representation maps every pure qubit state to a unique point on the surface of a unit sphere. The north pole is |0⟩, the south pole is |1⟩, and the equator holds all equal superpositions.

|0⟩ North |1⟩ South |+⟩ Equator |−⟩ Equator

Superposition

When θ = π/2, the qubit is in an equal superposition — neither |0⟩ nor |1⟩, but genuinely both. The state |+⟩ = (|0⟩ + |1⟩)/√2 sits on the positive X-axis of the sphere, pointing outward from the equator.

|+⟩ = 1/√2 · (|0⟩ + |1⟩)
Equal probability of measuring 0 or 1

Quantum Phase

The angle φ encodes the relative phase between the |0⟩ and |1⟩ components. Phase has no classical analogue — it doesn't affect measurement probabilities alone, but it matters when gates are applied and for interference effects.

eiφ = cos φ + i·sin φ
Phase rotates the state around the Z-axis

03 · Quantum Gates

Rotations on the sphere

Every single-qubit gate is a rotation of the Bloch vector. Apply them above and watch the transformation.

X
Pauli-X · Quantum NOT
Rotates the state by 180° around the X-axis. The quantum analogue of a classical NOT gate — it flips |0⟩ to |1⟩ and vice versa.
X = [0 1]
     [1 0]
Y
Pauli-Y
Rotates 180° around the Y-axis. Combines a bit flip and a phase flip. Maps |0⟩ → i|1⟩ and |1⟩ → −i|0⟩.
Y = [0 −i]
     [i  0]
Z
Pauli-Z · Phase Flip
Rotates 180° around the Z-axis. Leaves |0⟩ unchanged but maps |1⟩ → −|1⟩. A pure phase operation — invisible to Z-basis measurement.
Z = [1  0]
     [0 −1]
H
Hadamard Gate
The most important single-qubit gate. Rotates 180° around the X+Z diagonal axis, creating equal superposition: |0⟩ → |+⟩ and |1⟩ → |−⟩.
H = 1/√2 · [1  1]
            [1 −1]
S
Phase Gate · S Gate
A 90° rotation around the Z-axis (quarter turn). Maps |+⟩ → |i⟩. It is the square root of Z: applying S twice equals applying Z once.
S = [1 0]
     [0 i]
T
π/8 Gate · T Gate
A 45° rotation around the Z-axis. The square root of S. Together with H, the T gate forms a universal gate set for quantum computation.
T = [1      0]
     [0 eiπ/4]
04 · Measurement

Collapse of the state

Measuring a qubit forces it to "choose" — collapsing from superposition into a definite classical value.

|0⟩
100%
|1⟩
0%
P(0) = cos²(θ/2)
P(1) = sin²(θ/2)
P(0) + P(1) = 1

Born's Rule & Wavefunction Collapse

The probability of measuring a qubit in state |0⟩ is given by Born's rule: the squared magnitude of the amplitude. For the Bloch sphere parameterization, this is simply cos²(θ/2).

When θ = 0° (north pole), we measure |0⟩ with 100% certainty. When θ = 180° (south pole), we always get |1⟩. At the equator (θ = 90°) the two outcomes are equally probable.

Key insight: The azimuthal angle φ — which encodes phase — has no effect on measurement probabilities in the Z-basis. Phase only becomes observable through interference when multiple qubits or multiple gates are involved.
Irreversibility: Measurement is the only non-unitary operation in quantum mechanics. After collapse, the phase information encoded in φ is permanently lost.
